# QuantLab Risk Engine & Position Sizing Architecture (Part 14)

## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

The **QuantLab Risk Engine** is an institutional-grade quantitative risk management and position sizing module designed specifically for Indian equity markets.

Given an upstream `BUY`, `HOLD`, or `SELL` signal generated by the **Cross-Check / Signal Engine (Part 13)**, the Risk Engine calculates:
1. **Suggested Capital Allocation & Quantity**: The maximum permissible investment amount and number of shares without breaching risk bounds.
2. **Dynamic Stop Price & Stop Distance**: Methodologically derived invalidation points based on ATR multiples, volatility bands, or support/resistance.
3. **Estimated Downside & Position Risk**: Quantified monetary loss ($|\text{Entry} - \text{Stop}| \times \text{Quantity}$) and downside expressed as a percentage of total portfolio value.
4. **Multi-Constraint Resolution**: Transparent binding of capital allocation to the most restrictive applicable constraint.
5. **Drawdown Protection & Volatility Scaling**: Dynamic risk reduction during portfolio drawdowns and high-volatility regimes.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Strict Analytical Risk Discipline:**
> - The Risk Engine **does not** generate BUY/SELL trading signals.
> - The Risk Engine **does not** route broker orders or execute trades.
> - The Risk Engine **never** claims positions are "safe", "guaranteed", or "risk-free".
> - All calculations are strictly **Point-in-Time Safe** ($T_{\text{avail}} \le T$).

## 2. Mathematical Formulations & Sizing Methodologies

### 2.1 Stop Distance & Position Risk Amount

For long positions:
$$\text{Stop Distance} = \max(0.01, \text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop Price})$$
$$\text{Stop Distance \%} = \frac{\text{Stop Distance}}{\text{Entry Price}}$$

For short positions:
$$\text{Stop Distance} = \max(0.01, \text{Stop Price} - \text{Entry Price})$$

$$\text{Position Risk Amount (INR)} = \text{Stop Distance} \times \text{Quantity}$$
$$\text{Position Risk \%} = \frac{\text{Position Risk Amount}}{\text{Portfolio Value}}$$

---

### 2.2 Sizing Strategies

#### 1. Fixed Risk Sizing (`FIXED_RISK`)
Allocates shares such that the maximum potential loss on the position equals the investor's risk budget:
$$\text{Risk Budget Capital} = \text{Portfolio Value} \times \text{Max Position Risk \%}$$
$$\text{Quantity} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Risk Budget Capital}}{\text{Stop Distance}} \right\rfloor$$
$$\text{Unconstrained Capital} = \text{Quantity} \times \text{Entry Price}$$

#### 2. Fixed Allocation Sizing (`FIXED_ALLOCATION`)
Allocates a fixed percentage of total portfolio capital:
$$\text{Target Capital} = \text{Portfolio Value} \times \text{Max Position Allocation \%}$$
$$\text{Quantity} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Target Capital}}{\text{Entry Price}} \right\rfloor$$

#### 3. Volatility-Adjusted Sizing (`VOLATILITY_ADJUSTED`)
Scales position size inversely with security realized volatility or Model 3 expected volatility:
$$\text{Vol Scalar} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{target}}}{\max(\sigma_{\text{min}}, \sigma_i)}$$
$$\text{Adjusted Risk Budget} = \text{Portfolio Value} \times \text{Max Position Risk \%} \times \text{Vol Scalar}$$
$$\text{Quantity} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Adjusted Risk Budget}}{\text{Stop Distance}} \right\rfloor$$

#### 4. Portfolio-Aware Sizing (`PORTFOLIO_AWARE`)
Penalizes positions that have high correlation ($\rho_{i,j} > 0.60$) with existing portfolio positions:
$$\text{Penalty Multiplier} = \max(0.20, 1.0 - (\max(\rho) - 0.60) \times 1.5)$$
$$\text{Adjusted Quantity} = \left\lfloor \text{Base Quantity} \times \text{Penalty Multiplier} \right\rfloor$$

## 3. Portfolio Risk & Covariance

The portfolio annualized volatility is calculated using the covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}$ and weight vector $\mathbf{w}$:
$$\sigma_p^2 = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}$$
$$\sigma_p = \sqrt{\sigma_p^2 \times 252}$$

Where $\mathbf{\Sigma}_{i,j} = \text{Cov}(R_i, R_j)$ calculated across historical overlapping trading days $T_{\text{lookback}}$.

## 4. Constraint Engine & Limiting Resolution

Every candidate allocation passes through a multi-constraint ladder:

| Constraint | Description | Default Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Max Single Security** | Hard ceiling on single asset weight | 10% (Moderate) |
| **Max Sector Exposure** | Cap on aggregate sector allocation | 25% (Moderate) |
| **Cash Buffer** | Minimum uninvested liquidity retained | 5% (Moderate) |
| **Total Risk Budget** | Sum of position downside risks $\le \text{Max Portfolio Risk}$ | 5% total risk |
| **Drawdown Scaling** | Multiplier $\le 1.0$ applied when portfolio is in drawdown | Active if Drawdown $> 5\%$ |
| **ADV Liquidity Limit** | Position size $\le 5\%$ of Average Daily Volume | $5\%$ ADV |

$$\text{Suggested Allocation} = \min(\text{Unconstrained}, \text{Single Security Cap}, \text{Sector Room}, \text{Available Cash}, \text{ADV Cap}) \times \text{Drawdown Multiplier}$$

The engine logs the exact **Limiting Constraint** in machine-readable `RiskTrace` JSON for complete auditability.

## 6. Point-in-Time & Lookahead Bias Invariance

The risk pipeline guarantees PIT compliance through:
1. **Timestamp Gating**: Only prices, covariance matrices, and portfolio states available at $T_{\text{as\_of}}$ are consumed.
2. **Invariance Verification**: Unit tests verify that altering market prices or signals at $T + 1$ does not alter risk assessments or sizing at $T$.
3. **Data Quality Gating**: Any missing price, zero volatility, or malformed portfolio state immediately rejects allocation with `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` rather than returning ungrounded sizes.
